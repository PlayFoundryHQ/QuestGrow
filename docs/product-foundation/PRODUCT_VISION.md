# QuestGrow Product Vision

## Problem

Young children thrive on routine, but routines are hard to establish. Parents
end up nagging, negotiating, and re-explaining the same expectations every day:
brush your teeth, get dressed, tidy your toys, get ready for school. This is
exhausting for parents and demotivating for children, and it frames everyday
responsibility as conflict.

Existing tools do not fit:

- **Chore / reward-chart apps** are thin digital checklists. They let children
  tap "done" and score themselves, which teaches gaming the system rather than
  doing the task.
- **Habit trackers** are built for literate, self-motivated adults.
- **Kids' games** compete for screen time — the opposite of the goal.

## Vision

QuestGrow turns everyday routines into a gentle game that a 3–8 year old
child plays *in the real world*, guided by a parent who stays in control of
what counts.

The child opens the app, sees today's quests as friendly pictures, goes and
does the real thing, comes back to mark it, and — once a parent confirms the
ones that need confirming — gets a small burst of celebration and watches
their progress grow. Over days and weeks, doing the routine becomes its own
reward and its own habit.

## Target users

- **Primary child user:** approximately 3–8 years old. The initial reference
  child is around 5–6, but the product is **age-adaptive** — vocabulary, text
  volume, iconography, interaction and task complexity, reading requirements,
  reward presentation, and expected independence all shift with configured age.
- **Primary account holder:** a parent or caregiver. The app lives on the
  parent's phone / device. The parent does all configuration and verification.
- Multiple children per parent account is supported in the data model from
  day one, even if the first release focuses on one.

## Product principles (summary)

The [Manifesto](./MANIFESTO.md) and the 24 numbered
[CORE_PRINCIPLES](./CORE_PRINCIPLES.md) govern the product. In vision terms
they reduce to three commitments:

1. **The child side stays tiny.** Simplicity is a feature we defend, not a
   starting point we grow out of.
2. **The real world is the product.** Screen time is a means; real-world
   action and habit formation are the outcome we optimize for.
3. **Trust is structural.** The parent-verification model is not a setting —
   it is the backbone that keeps QuestGrow honest.

## What success looks like

- A child asks to "do their quests" without being told.
- A parent spends less time nagging and a minute or two a day in the app.
- Routine behaviors (the specific quests a family chose) happen more reliably
  after a few weeks.
- Total child screen time attributable to QuestGrow stays low — and we treat
  a *decrease* over time as healthy, not as churn.

## Non-goals

See [Manifesto → What QuestGrow must never become](./MANIFESTO.md). Explicitly
out of scope: child-to-child social features, location tracking, screen-time
enforcement / device locking, punitive mechanics, ad-supported or
engagement-maximizing monetization.

## Long-term direction

Once the core loop is proven, optional depth can be layered on *for the child*
without adding child-side complexity: collectible characters, evolving worlds,
storybooks unlocked by consistency, seasonal themes. All optional, all
parent-toggleable, none required to get value.
